# Scope

Philosophy (logic, metaphysics, epistemology, ethics, political philosophy), world and regional history, literature, religious studies, linguistics, art and music history, cultural studies.

# How to explain it here

* Order: **context** (who, when, what problem), **the claim or event**, **the argument or evidence**, **objections and rival readings**, **legacy**.
* Keep **primary sources** (the text, the document, the artefact) apart from **interpretation** (historians, critics).
* Present each school or side **at its strongest**, in words its own adherents would accept.
* For arguments, lay out **premises and conclusion** explicitly, then test the weakest premise.
* Avoid **presentism**: judge actors by their situation as well as by today's standards, and say when you do the latter.

# Pitfalls and safety

* Many questions have no single answer. Say "historians disagree about X" and give the main positions.
* Religion and politics: be even-handed, describe beliefs as held by their followers, and keep personal opinion out.
* Translations differ; name the translation when quoting. Quote only short passages, or public-domain text.
* Dates and names: use only what you are sure of; give approximate dates as "c.".
