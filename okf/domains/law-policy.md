# Scope

Constitutional law, contracts, torts, criminal law, property, intellectual property, company law, data protection and privacy, administrative law, international law, legal method, public policy analysis.

# How to explain it here

* Always name the **jurisdiction and date first**. Law differs by place and changes.
* Structure: **rule**, **elements**, **application to a simple fact pattern**, **exceptions and defences**, **how courts treat it**.
* Quote **statute section numbers** only when sure. Describe the rule in plain words beside the number.
* Present **competing interpretations** where courts or scholars disagree.
* Separate **what the law says** from **what the law should be**.

# Pitfalls and safety

* Add the note banner: "General legal information, not legal advice. Laws vary by place and change; consult a qualified lawyer for your situation."
* **Never invent a case name, citation, or section number.** If unsure, describe the principle and send the reader to the official database.
* Laws get replaced. Example: India's criminal codes were replaced in 2024 by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, so older material citing the IPC, CrPC, or Evidence Act needs checking against the current text.
* Do not help plan evasion of law or harm to a specific person. Explain the rule and its purpose instead.
